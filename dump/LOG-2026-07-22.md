This regimen. This process of flowing my thoughts and actions at such a granular and frequent level... feels unsettling. 

Unnatural even.

It could be because I never journaled much when I was younger. This feels like journaling.

This is not journaling.

The objective here is to provide as much information to a agentic system, and have it organize that information into a different model. A model that complements the deficiencies of the human brain. I am not sure what that model is. I would assume that it would be mostly unique to each person.

Whatever the agentic system may be, it fails without sufficient information of the person, and I believe the best communication of this information is what we are doing right now. Well I shouldn't say best, but rather most flexible, but I think the two are interchangeable here.

Think of the alternatives. 

What if there was no objectively best communication structure. What if the best communication structure is as unique and subjective as the systems we aide our brains with. Consider the agenda, a system used to help a forgetful brain remember it's time commitments. To best reap the benefits of the agenda, we must adhere to communicating the "agenda way". There is nothing wrong about communicating via a 4x7, 7x24, or 1x48 grid. Many people have found great success by doing so, but there are also many people who find agendas difficult to use, and perhaps even increasing their cognitive stress.

Of course these agenda haters will start searching around for a new system. Whatever this new system, It is unlikely that they can communicate with it via the "agenda way". They will likely have to learn a new set of rituals.

 A new regimen.
 
 A new form of communication.

Another gamble, where some will find success, and others will keep roaming the world for a miracle.

Okay. That is how life goes? We keep searching until we find what we are looking for. Until we find some unique way of communicating that fits us best.

Sure. Lets assume we all get our fairytale ending. 

Now who is to say that an agenda will always be helpful? Even if you did master the art of using an agenda, there are many situations where it can be counter productive. What do you do when you feel it would be helpful to also write down your entire shopping list for the grocery run you scheduled? It would be real tough to write that within a 4x7 grid. Maybe a agenda with each day as a page? What if your list needs two pages? What about four?

The two outcomes here are always this: You keep changing the agenda until it is no longer an agenda, or you accept that the agenda system is a poor aide for your new problems.

Once again, you roam.

You experiment. You fail. You succeed. You grow. You face new problems. 

Once again, you roam, because the way we think is not static by nature.

There may be hidden and important utility in this cyclical process, but I do wonder what is the opportunity cost. We spend a-lot of time relearning ways to communicate and manage our thoughts. Time that could have been spent thinking without restrictions.

I can only think of one remaining alternative. A single system that grows and adapts with our changing cognitive needs. A system that requires us to learn and master only one method of communicating our thoughts. A form of communication not tailored to a specific problem but tailored to the very essence of thinking.

Yes. 

I think this is just called journaling.
